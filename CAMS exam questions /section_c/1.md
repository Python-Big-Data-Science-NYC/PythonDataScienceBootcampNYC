### C.1 What Makes an Effective Compliance Program and the Core Pillars of the AFC Program

---

**Question C.1.1**
Under U.S. Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) requirements (31 CFR §1020.210) as amended by FinCEN's 2016 CDD Final Rule, which of the following correctly identifies the five minimum required pillars of a financial institution's AML compliance program?

A. Internal controls; a designated compliance officer; ongoing employee training; independent testing; customer due diligence (including beneficial ownership identification)

B. Annual board resolutions; a quarterly sanctions report; a law enforcement liaison desk; a digital asset custodian; automated wire screening

C. Simplified due diligence for all customers; waiver of PEP screening for domestic clients; outsourced monitoring to a third-party vendor; a customer ombudsman; annual profit-sharing with regulators

D. Internal controls; a designated compliance officer; outsourced customer onboarding; independent testing; monthly currency transaction reports

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The BSA requires five pillars: (1) internal controls, (2) designated compliance officer, (3) ongoing employee training, (4) independent testing, and (5) customer due diligence (including beneficial ownership identification per the 2016 CDD Final Rule).

**Question C.1.2**
A federal banking regulator has issued a Consent Order against a mid-sized bank citing: transaction monitoring thresholds calibrated once at implementation five years ago and never revised; BSA Officer position vacant for 11 months; independent testing conducted by the same team that designed the monitoring scenarios; KYC files for 34% of high-risk accounts missing required EDD documentation; and SAR narratives for 60% of filed SARs lacking sufficient detail. What is the most accurate characterization of the root cause?

A. The root cause is a technology deficiency requiring system replacement

B. The five findings collectively indicate a systemic governance and organizational failure requiring structural changes to accountability, resources, and board-level engagement with the AML program

C. Each finding is an isolated operational incident with no single root cause

D. The root cause is insufficient SAR volume; the bank should increase SAR filings

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The findings indicate systemic governance failures: lack of senior management commitment (vacant BSA Officer for 11 months), inadequate resources (missing EDD documentation, poor SAR quality), institutional independence failures (audit team self-reviewing its own scenarios), and no program maintenance culture (monitoring never recalibrated). These require structural changes, not just technology upgrades.

### C.2 Roles and Responsibilities of the Three Lines of Defense, and the BSA Officer/MLRO

---

**Question C.2.1**
A bank's internal audit team (Third Line of Defense) is asked by the Chief Compliance Officer to assist in redesigning the bank's AML transaction monitoring scenario library because the compliance team lacks technical expertise. The internal audit team agrees and spends three months co-designing 14 new monitoring scenarios. Six months later, the same audit team conducts an independent test of the system and concludes all 14 new scenarios are effectively calibrated. What fundamental governance failure has occurred?

A. The Chief Compliance Officer violated GDPR by sharing transaction monitoring data without customer consent

B. The internal audit team has compromised its independence by designing the same controls it subsequently audited; an audit team cannot objectively test controls it helped create, violating the Three Lines of Defense framework

C. The compliance team acted appropriately by delegating technical design work to internal audit

D. The governance violation can be cured by having the Chief Compliance Officer sign an attestation confirming the audit findings are independent

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The Three Lines of Defense framework requires the Third Line (Internal Audit) to maintain independence from the functions being audited. By designing controls and then auditing them, the audit team cannot be objective about its own design decisions.

---

**Question C.2.2**
Which two functions correctly align with the Three Lines of Defense in AML governance?

A. First Line: Business operations and front-line staff who own customer relationships

B. Second Line: Independent Audit Department that designs transaction monitoring rules

C. Second Line: Compliance Department and AML Officer providing oversight and guidance

D. Third Line: Business line executives who approve every transaction

**Correct Answers: A and C (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** First Line = business operations (customer relationships, transaction initiation). Second Line = compliance/risk management (oversight, guidance, policies). Third Line = internal audit (independent assurance). The independent audit department should not design controls; that is a Second Line function.

**Question C.2.3**
Which two responsibilities belong directly to a financial institution's Board of Directors?

A. Approving the AML compliance program and its material changes

B. Filing every Suspicious Activity Report individually

C. Reviewing every customer transaction over $1,000

D. Providing oversight and holding senior management accountable for AML effectiveness

**Correct Answers: A and D (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** The Board of Directors is responsible for approving the AML compliance program (and material changes) and providing oversight, holding senior management accountable for AML effectiveness. Day-to-day operational tasks (filing SARs, reviewing transactions) are management responsibilities.

### C.4 Enterprise Risk Assessment: How Inherent Risk Is Identified, and Controls Used to Assess Residual Risk

---

**Question C.4.1**
A mid-sized regional bank is preparing its annual Enterprise-Wide Risk Assessment (EWRA). The assessment team calculates the following inherent risk scores: Retail banking: 6.2/10, Commercial lending: 4.1/10, Private banking: 8.7/10. After applying mitigating controls, residual risk scores are: retail 4.4/10, commercial 2.8/10, private banking 6.9/10. The EWRA methodology defines residual risk above 6.0 as "high residual risk" requiring escalation to the board and independent audit prioritization. Which of the following correctly describes the bank's next required steps?

A. The EWRA is complete once residual scores are documented. No further action is required as long as the bank's controls remain in place.

B. The retail and private banking business lines both require board-level escalation.

C. Only the private banking business line requires board-level escalation, because it is the only line with a residual risk score exceeding the 6.0 high-risk threshold. D. All three business lines require immediate account closure of all high-risk relationships.

### C.5 The Importance of a Risk-Based Approach (RBA) and Building Controls Commensurate with Risks

---

**Question C.5.1**
The FATF's risk-based approach (RBA) to AML/CFT compliance allows financial institutions to differentiate the intensity of their due diligence measures. What is the fundamental justification for applying a risk-based approach rather than a purely rules-based, "one-size-fits-all" approach?

A. Regulators prefer rules-based approaches because they are easier to audit

B. An RBA concentrates resources and controls on areas of highest risk, making the overall system more effective and efficient, while proportionate measures are applied to lower-risk situations without creating unnecessary burdens

C. An RBA eliminates all due diligence requirements for low-risk customers

D. The RBA is only applicable to correspondent banking relationships, not retail customers

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The RBA allocates resources and controls proportionately to risk levels—higher intensity for higher risk, proportionate measures for lower risk—making the system more effective and efficient than a uniform approach.

---

**Question C.5.2**
Under FATF and CAMS standards, Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is not a fixed checklist but a flexible, risk-proportionate set of measures. A compliance officer is designing EDD procedures for three distinct high-risk scenarios:
- Scenario X: Domestic PEP (provincial-level) opening a standard personal savings account with monthly deposits of $5,000 from salary
- Scenario Y: Foreign PEP (senior minister, high-corruption country) establishing private banking to manage $45 million portfolio from "historical business income" in same sector as ministry
- Scenario Z: Foreign corporate client from FATF Black List jurisdiction applying for $2 million trade finance for dual-use electronic components

Which statement best describes the correct differentiation of EDD intensity?

A. All three scenarios require identical EDD procedures because all are classified as high risk

B. Scenario X requires proportionate EDD (verify PEP status, source of salary funds, board approval). Scenario Y requires most intensive EDD (full source of wealth investigation, corroborated documentation, senior management sign-off, transaction-by-transaction review). Scenario Z requires EDD focused on dual-use goods end-user certification, trade finance documentation chain, and sanctions screening. The RBA requires calibrating EDD depth to specific risk drivers

C. Scenario Z requires no EDD because it involves a corporate client rather than a PEP

D. EDD cannot be differentiated; regulatory consistency requires applying the same maximum-intensity EDD to all three scenarios

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The RBA requires calibrating EDD intensity to the specific risk drivers present. Scenario Y (foreign PEP, high-corruption jurisdiction, large portfolio, ministry-sector-linked funds) requires the most intensive EDD. Scenario Z requires transaction-specific EDD focused on proliferation financing risks. Scenario X requires proportionate, lower-intensity EDD.

**Question C.10.1**
A compliance officer at a U.S. bank has completed an investigation into a corporate account and has reached the conclusion that there is reasonable suspicion of money laundering. The compliance committee is divided: three members believe a SAR should be filed; two members (including the relationship manager) argue the evidence is "not conclusive" and filing would "damage the relationship." The BSA Officer decides to support the non-filing position. Which of the following most accurately describes the legal and regulatory risk of this decision?

A. The BSA Officer's decision is legally sound. SAR filing requires conclusive evidence of money laundering; reasonable suspicion is insufficient.

B. The BSA Officer's decision creates material regulatory risk. The SAR filing standard in the U.S. (and under FATF standards globally) is "reasonable suspicion"—not certainty or conclusive proof. Failure to file when the reasonable suspicion standard is met exposes the institution to regulatory sanctions and the BSA Officer to personal liability.

C. The BSA Officer can resolve the situation by filing a CTR instead of a SAR.

D. The institution should file the SAR but notify the customer simultaneously to maintain the commercial relationship.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The SAR filing standard is "reasonable suspicion," not conclusive proof. Failure to file when reasonable suspicion exists exposes the institution to regulatory sanctions (including civil money penalties), the BSA Officer to personal liability, and potentially criminal prosecution. Commercial relationship considerations are never a legally permissible basis for non-filing.

A bank's compliance team has filed an STR on a customer. The customer calls his relationship manager asking why a wire transfer has been delayed. The relationship manager tells him the compliance team is "reviewing some transactions" on his account. Which statements accurately identify a tipping-off violation?

A. The relationship manager's statement constitutes tipping-off; alerting the customer that he is under investigation is a violation regardless of whether a formal STR has been filed

B. Disclosing the STR to the bank's own legal department constitutes tipping-off

C. Sharing STR information with another bank in an inter-institution AML investigation is never permissible

D. The relationship manager's statement is a tipping-off violation even though he did not mention the STR by name

**Correct Answers: A and D (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Tipping-off includes disclosing the existence of compliance scrutiny of a specific account to the account holder, even without mentioning the SAR by name. Internal legal department sharing on a need-to-know basis is generally permissible. Inter-institution sharing under 314(b) with safe harbor is permissible.

Which two actions would constitute illegal "tipping-off" under AML laws?

A. Telling a customer that a SAR has been filed on their account

B. Disclosing to a third party that an investigation is being considered

C. Filing a SAR in good faith with the relevant FIU

D. Sharing a SAR internally with the compliance committee on a need-to-know basis

**Correct Answers: A and B (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Tipping-off includes telling a customer a SAR has been filed or disclosing to a third party that an investigation is being considered. Good-faith SAR filing and internal need-to-know sharing with the compliance committee are permissible and protected.

Which training design principles are most critical for regulatory compliance?

A. Training must be tailored to the specific roles, responsibilities, and ML/TF risks faced by each employee group. Frontline tellers need cash structuring and CTR training; private bankers need PEP, source of wealth, and EDD training; board members need governance oversight training

B. All employees must complete an identical training program for consistent institutional coverage

C. The IT infrastructure team has no material exposure to AML risk and can be exempted

D. Training frequency and content must be documented and presented to the board on at least an annual basis, with evidence that training effectiveness has been assessed

A bank designs an annual AML training module delivering identical technical transaction monitoring training to the board of directors, frontline tellers, and facilities maintenance staff. Why does this fail to meet regulatory and CAMS expectations?

B. Training must be customized and targeted to the specific roles, duties, and operational risks faced by distinct segments of employees

Which of the following best describes the typical reporting structure for an AML compliance function in a well-governed financial institution?
