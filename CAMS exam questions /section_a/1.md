### A.1 Definitions of AML, CFT, Sanctions, Fraud, ABC, and Tax Evasion

---

**Question A.1.1**
A compliance analyst is reviewing a transaction that involves funds originating from charitable donations used to support a designated terrorist organization. The analyst must determine the correct regulatory classification. Which definition most accurately describes this activity?

A. Money laundering, because funds are being moved through the financial system
B. Terrorist financing, because funds may come from legitimate sources but are directed toward terrorist acts
C. Fraud, because charitable donations were misrepresented to donors
D. Tax evasion, because the charity likely did not report the donations

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Terrorist financing (TF) involves the collection or provision of funds to support terrorist activities. Unlike money laundering, TF often involves funds from legitimate sources (such as charitable donations) that are diverted to terrorist purposes. The concealment objective in TF is directed at the intended use (financing a terrorist act) rather than the origin of the funds.

---

**Question A.1.2**
A financial intelligence analyst is preparing a briefing for a bank's board on the key operational differences between money laundering (ML) and terrorist financing (TF). Which statement most accurately describes a critical distinction between the two financial crime types?

A. Money laundering always involves large sums of money, while terrorist financing always involves small sums
B. In money laundering, the criminal objective is to conceal the illicit origin of the proceeds and make them appear legitimate; in terrorist financing, the funds may originate from entirely legal sources and the concealment objective is directed at the intended use rather than the origin
C. Terrorist financing is only relevant for banks with operations in conflict zones; domestic retail banks have no TF exposure
D. Both ML and TF involve the same three-stage cycle (placement, layering, integration); the only difference is the geographic location of the criminal network

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** This distinction is critical because standard AML controls focused on suspicious origins may fail to detect TF involving clean-source funds. TF can involve small amounts from many donors (crowdfunding) and the funds may be entirely legitimate, making detection through traditional suspicious transaction monitoring difficult.

**Question A.1.3**
A European bank's legal team is reviewing whether a recent enforcement action against a corporate subsidiary triggers liability under the EU Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive (6AMLD). The subsidiary was found to have knowingly processed wire transfers totaling €12 million in proceeds derived from tax fraud, which is designated as a predicate offense under 6AMLD. Which statement most accurately describes the subsidiary's exposure?

A. The subsidiary faces no criminal exposure because 6AMLD only harmonizes predicate offenses for natural persons, not corporate entities
B. The subsidiary faces criminal liability for money laundering as a legal person under 6AMLD, as the Directive harmonizes 22 predicate offenses including tax crimes and explicitly extends criminal liability to legal persons
C. The subsidiary is only liable if the individual directors are also convicted; corporate liability cannot exist independently
D. Tax fraud was not included in the 6AMLD harmonized list of predicate offenses

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** 6AMLD explicitly extends criminal liability to legal persons (corporations). The corporation itself, not only its individual officers, can be prosecuted and subject to criminal sanctions such as fines, debarment from public contracts, and dissolution.

### A.2 Financial Systems, Economic and Social Consequences of ML, and Risks/Impacts of AFC Violations

---

**Question A.2.1**
A major international bank terminates its correspondent banking relationships with all respondent banks headquartered in Sub-Saharan Africa, citing "high risk" of the region without conducting individual assessments. What is the most significant systemic consequence of this indiscriminate de-risking approach?

A. The respondent banks will simply switch to a different SWIFT messaging code without any disruption
B. Legitimate businesses, remittance corridors, and financial institutions in developing economies lose access to the global financial system, driving transactions into unregulated, informal channels that are harder to monitor
C. The correspondent bank will automatically receive a reduced FATF mutual evaluation score for its actions
D. The respondent banks will merge with local central banks, which is the desired regulatory outcome

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Indiscriminate de-risking forces legitimate economic activity into unregulated, informal channels that are less transparent and harder to monitor, paradoxically increasing overall money laundering risk rather than reducing it.

**Question A.2.2**
A country has enacted comprehensive AML/CFT legal frameworks and a functioning FIU that receives over 30,000 STRs annually. However, the FIU's intelligence has never been disseminated to law enforcement, the country has secured zero asset forfeitures, and courts have not prosecuted a single money laundering case in seven years. What is the most likely consequence for this country?

A. The country will receive a "Compliant" rating on Technical Compliance and "High" on Effectiveness
B. The country will receive "Largely Compliant" on Technical Compliance but "Low" on Effectiveness for Immediate Outcomes including IO.6, IO.7, and IO.8
C. The country will be immediately expelled from FATF
D. The country will be automatically transferred to the UN Security Council for direct sanctions

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** FATF Mutual Evaluations assess both Technical Compliance (laws on paper) and Effectiveness (actual results). A country can have perfect laws but receive low effectiveness ratings if intelligence is not used, investigations don't occur, and assets are not confiscated.

### A.3 Predicate Crimes Underlying Financial Crime

---

**Question A.3.1**
A federal prosecutor is considering charging an individual with both wire fraud (the predicate offense) and money laundering under 18 U.S.C. §1956 for the subsequent movement of the fraud proceeds through a shell company network. The defense argues that charging both constitutes double jeopardy. Which statement correctly assesses this argument?

A. The double jeopardy argument is correct; charging both violates the Fifth Amendment
B. The double jeopardy argument is without merit; a money laundering charge and a predicate offense charge involve legally distinct elements and distinct criminal acts
C. The prosecutor must choose one charge or the other under federal law
D. Double jeopardy applies only if both charges carry the same maximum sentence

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Money laundering and predicate offenses involve legally distinct elements (committing the underlying crime vs. conducting financial transactions with the proceeds). Prosecuting both simultaneously does not violate double jeopardy because each offense requires proof of a different element.

---

**Question A.3.2**
A money launderer processes drug trafficking proceeds through a series of shell companies and real estate purchases. Under FATF Recommendation 3, which approach to predicate offenses is preferred?

A. A narrow list of exactly twelve designated predicate offenses that every country must adopt verbatim
B. The all-crimes approach, under which money laundering applies to proceeds from all criminal acts defined as serious under national law
C. Only drug trafficking and terrorism-related offenses qualify as predicate crimes under international standards
D. Predicate offense lists are entirely optional; each country may choose not to designate any predicate offenses

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** FATF Recommendation 3 requires countries to apply the crime of money laundering to the widest range of predicate offenses, preferably adopting the "all-crimes" approach covering all serious offenses under national law.

**Question A.3.3**
An organized crime group controls a network of nail salons generating approximately $4 million per year in illicit narcotics proceeds. The accountant deposits cash at 14 different branch locations across four banks, keeping each deposit between $7,500 and $9,800. Funds are consolidated into a holding LLC, which issues fictitious management-fee invoices to all 14 salons. The LLC uses aggregated funds to purchase residential rental properties. Which statement correctly identifies the predicate offense implications?

A. The structured deposits constitute the only predicate offense; all other activities are legitimate business operations
B. Narcotics trafficking is the predicate offense; the invoicing and property purchases constitute money laundering of those proceeds
C. There is no predicate offense because the funds have been legitimized through the LLC structure
D. Only the fictitious invoicing constitutes a predicate offense; the narcotics proceeds are not relevant

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Narcotics trafficking is a serious predicate offense under FATF standards and most national laws. The subsequent activities (structuring, invoicing, property purchases) constitute money laundering of the drug proceeds.

### A.4 How ML, TF, Sanctions, Bribery and Corruption, Tax Evasion, and Fraud Manifest Across Sectors

---

**Question A.4.1**
A compliance officer at a trade finance bank reviews a letter of credit supporting the import of pharmaceutical raw materials from Country A (high corruption) to Country B (FATF-compliant). The invoice shows $1,200 per kilogram, but the international market price is $180 per kilogram. The exporter was incorporated 11 days ago with no verifiable manufacturing infrastructure. What is the primary TBML technique being used?

A. Under-invoicing by the exporter, enabling the importer to sell goods at market price and retain an untaxed cash surplus
B. Over-invoicing by the exporter, enabling the transfer of excess value from Country B to Country A disguised as a legitimate commercial payment
C. Phantom shipping, where no goods actually exist
D. Black Market Peso Exchange structural manipulation

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Over-invoicing (inflated prices) allows the transfer of excess value from the importing country (B) to the exporting country (A) disguised as legitimate commercial payments, moving illicit funds from a FATF-compliant jurisdiction to a high-risk secrecy haven.

---

**Question A.4.2**
A logistics company operating within a Free Trade Zone has processed 340 container shipments declared as "industrial parts" with uniform invoice values of $30,000 per container regardless of cargo type. Physical inspection reveals one container had industrial parts, one had household furniture, one was empty, and one contained electronics worth $400,000. Bills of lading have been altered. Which findings most directly constitute money laundering red flags?

A. The uniform invoice value regardless of actual cargo type, and the presence of an empty container suggesting phantom shipping
B. The free trade zone location, which automatically exempts all transactions from AML scrutiny
C. The nominee director structure, which is a standard corporate governance practice
D. The use of bills of lading, which are standard shipping documents

**Correct Answers: A and C (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Uniform invoice values regardless of cargo is a hallmark of TBML through price manipulation. Empty containers suggest phantom shipping (false documentation without physical movement of goods of commercial value).

**Question A.4.3**
A logistics firm operating inside a Middle Eastern Free Trade Zone alters customs manifests and bills of lading for a shipment of consumer electronics, mislabeling the cargo as low-value plastic scrap to minimize duties and bypass inspection protocols. What inherent feature of FTZs creates this vulnerability?

A. The absolute prohibition of any financial transaction monitoring by local law enforcement
B. Relaxed regulatory oversight, minimal customs documentation inspections, and intense volume of re-exported goods
C. Direct integration with global central bank clearing networks that bypasses commercial banks
D. A standard legal requirement that all transactions within the zone be settled exclusively in physical cash

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Free Trade Zones often have relaxed oversight, minimal inspections, and high transaction volumes, creating vulnerabilities for trade-based money laundering and customs fraud.

How must the institution classify this relationship?

**Question A.7.1**
A commercial bank has maintained an account for eight years for a successful entrepreneur classified as medium risk. During a routine KYC refresh, a compliance analyst discovers the customer was appointed six months ago as Deputy Minister of Finance in a foreign government. The customer did not disclose this change. Which best describes the correct compliance response?

Within minutes, the ETH is deposited into a DeFi lending protocol as collateral for a $320,000 USDC loan, bridged to a second blockchain, converted to Monero (XMR), routed through a mixer, and bridged back as WETH two weeks later. Which findings require immediate action by the exchange?

**Question A.11.2**
A registered VASP receives a transfer of 4.2 Bitcoin from another VASP with no originator or beneficiary information attached. ### A.12 FC Risks Related to Gaming and Gambling

**Question A.12.1**
Over a single weekend at a Las Vegas casino, a player purchases $300,000 in chips using cash delivered in a duffel bag, plays blackjack for approximately 45 minutes with $1,500 in chips, then cashes out $297,000 for a casino check payable to his name. Which statements accurately describe the casino's obligations?

**Question A.17.1**
A regional bank's transaction monitoring flags an account held by the owner of a licensed nail salon. The account receives $18,000-$24,000 per month in cash deposits between $700-$1,400, plus weekly Venmo and Zelle payments from 28 different individuals. Every Monday, approximately 90% of the balance is withdrawn in cash. The account also pays utility bills for six different residential addresses. Which indicators most strongly suggest human trafficking?

A. The pattern of receiving payments from 28 different individuals and paying utility expenses for six residential addresses is consistent with a labor or sex trafficking operation where workers' earnings are deposited to a single controller account
B. The Monday cash withdrawal pattern (90% of weekly accumulation) suggests the controller is physically collecting and redistributing funds on a weekly cycle
C. The nail salon business fully explains the account activity
D. The LLC ownership of the residential properties is a standard real estate investment strategy

**Correct Answers: A and B (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Multiple individuals sending payments to a single controller account, combined with the controller paying utilities for multiple residential addresses, is a classic human trafficking indicator. The weekly cash withdrawal pattern suggests collection and redistribution of funds.

**Question A.17.2**
A compliance analyst reviews the account of a construction foreman that has received 150 separate peer-to-peer mobile payments ranging from $200-$600 from 35 different individuals with Hispanic surnames. Every Friday, the account holder withdraws 85%-95% of the balance (typically $8,000-$12,000) and pays utility bills for three high-density residential properties. The foreman files no corporate tax returns for a construction business. What does this pattern most strongly indicate?

A. Legitimate payroll arrangement for a small construction crew
B. Human trafficking for forced labor, where multiple victims' wages are deposited to a single controller account
C. Standard practice for subcontractor payment management
D. Tax evasion through unreported cash payments to workers

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The pattern—multiple individuals making regular payments to a single controller account, large cash withdrawals, and controller paying utilities for multiple residential properties—is highly indicative of human trafficking for forced labor.
