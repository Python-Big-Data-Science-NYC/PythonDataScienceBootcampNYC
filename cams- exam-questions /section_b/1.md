### B.1 FATF Role, Function, Regional Bodies, and Application of FATF Recommendations

---

**Question B.1.1**
Country Meridian has enacted comprehensive AML/CFT legal frameworks mapping to all 40 FATF Recommendations. The country has a functioning FIU receiving over 30,000 STRs annually. However, the FIU's intelligence has never been disseminated to law enforcement, the country has zero asset forfeitures, and courts have not prosecuted a single money laundering case in seven years. How will the Mutual Evaluation most likely be rated?

A. Largely Compliant on Technical Compliance; Low Effectiveness on multiple Immediate Outcomes, particularly IO.6 (financial intelligence used), IO.7 (ML investigated and prosecuted), and IO.8 (proceeds confiscated)

B. Non-Compliant on Technical Compliance because failure to prosecute demonstrates legislation does not actually meet FATF standards

C. Compliant on Technical Compliance and Largely Effective on Effectiveness because volume of STRs demonstrates system activity

D. Partially Compliant on Technical Compliance because legislation has not been effectively tested in courts

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** FATF Mutual Evaluations assess both Technical Compliance (laws on paper) and Effectiveness (actual results). A country can have perfect technical compliance but receive low effectiveness ratings if intelligence is not used by law enforcement, investigations don't occur, and assets are not confiscated. IO.6, IO.7, and IO.8 are directly implicated.

---

**Question B.1.2**
A FATF member nation undergoes a Mutual Evaluation. The assessment team notes the nation has passed comprehensive legislation covering all 40 Recommendations, but local courts have yielded zero money laundering convictions over five years due to systemic judicial corruption. How will the country's rating reflect this finding?

A. The country will receive a high compliance rating across all categories because legislation takes absolute priority over enforcement

B. The country will be rated compliant on Technical Compliance but will receive low marks for Effectiveness under the FATF Immediate Outcomes framework

C. The country will be immediately expelled from FATF without right of administrative appeal

D. The country will be automatically transferred to UN Security Council for direct legal sanctions

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The FATF Mutual Evaluation process separately assesses Technical Compliance (whether laws exist) and Effectiveness (whether laws achieve intended outcomes). Zero convictions despite comprehensive laws indicates low effectiveness, particularly for IO.7 (ML investigations and prosecutions).

---

**Question B.1.3**
In the FATF Mutual Evaluation process, Immediate Outcome 1 (IO.1) assesses: "Money laundering and terrorist financing risks are understood and, where appropriate, actions coordinated domestically." Which evidence best supports a positive rating for IO.1?

A. A thick legal code with criminalization of ML

B. A national risk assessment that is regularly updated and drives resource allocation

C. High number of STRs filed

D. Membership in the Egmont Group

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** IO.1 focuses on understanding risks and coordinating actions. A current, regularly updated national risk assessment that demonstrably drives resource allocation and policy decisions is the strongest evidence for a positive rating on this Immediate Outcome.

### B.2 United Nations Role and Sanctions Regimes

---

**Question B.2.1**
A bank handles a wire transfer originating from an entity that matches a name on the United Nations Security Council Consolidated Sanctions List. According to international mandates, what is the immediate mandatory operational response?

A. Process the transaction normally but submit a Suspicious Activity Report within 30 business days

B. Freeze the assets or funds immediately without prior notice to the entity, preventing any transfer, conversion, or movement of the asset

C. Return the funds immediately to the originating bank with an explanation notice

D. Contact the entity directly to request an explanation of the transaction's economic purpose

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Under UN Security Council sanctions resolutions, member states must freeze without delay the assets of designated individuals and entities. Financial institutions must freeze assets immediately without prior notice to the designated party.

---

**Question B.2.2**
A trade finance bank in Singapore is processing a letter of credit for export of industrial vacuum pumps, maraging steel tubing, and a coordinate measuring machine. The declared end-user is a private research institute in Country K, which is subject to active UN Security Council sanctions for its nuclear weapons development program. The declared end-use on the export license application is "quality control laboratory equipment for automotive manufacturing." The bank's dual-use goods screening system flags two items as controlled under EU and Wassenaar Arrangement lists. What is the most appropriate regulatory classification?

A. Standard trade finance risk requiring only enhanced customs documentation

B. Proliferation financing red flags; the bank must decline to issue the letter of credit, file an STR, and report to trade control authority under UNSCR 1540

C. The bank's only obligation is to verify the German exporter holds a valid EU export license

D. Proliferation financing obligations apply only to financial institutions in the weapons-originating country

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The combination of dual-use goods (maraging steel tubing, vacuum pumps, coordinate measuring machines are recognized components in uranium enrichment and nuclear weapons supply chains), destination country under UN sanctions, inconsistent end-use declaration, and multi-intermediary payment routing constitutes proliferation financing red flags requiring the bank to decline financing, file an STR, and notify the relevant export control authority under UNSCR 1540.

---

**Question B.2.3**
United Nations Security Council Resolution 1540 (2004) requires member states to do which of the following with respect to proliferation financing?

A. Establish and enforce effective controls over chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) weapons-related materials

B. Freeze all assets of any country developing nuclear weapons

C. Prohibit all cross-border trade in dual-use goods

D. Mandate that banks report all transactions involving any manufacturing sector

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** UNSCR 1540 requires member states to establish effective controls over CBRN weapons-related materials to prevent non-state actors from acquiring WMD-related materials and to prohibit non-state actors from manufacturing or acquiring such materials.

### B.3 Role of Regulators, Law Enforcement, and FIUs in Protecting Financial Integrity

---

**Question B.3.1**
An FIU in Country Alpha has identified a multi-layered embezzlement scheme where funds were routed through shell companies in four jurisdictions before being invested in private equity funds in Country Beta. Country Alpha and Country Beta have an active MLAT and are both Egmont Group members. Country Beta's banking secrecy laws prevent voluntary disclosure by its banks. Which mechanism provides the most appropriate pathway for Country Alpha to obtain account records?

A. Country Alpha's FIU should contact Country Beta's FIU through Egmont Secure Web for intelligence exchange while simultaneously submitting a formal MLAT request for bank account records admissible in criminal proceedings

B. Country Alpha can instruct its domestic financial institutions to demand account records directly from Country Beta's banks

C. Country Alpha's law enforcement should publicly name Country Beta's banks to pressure voluntary disclosure

D. The Egmont Group has binding enforcement authority to compel production of bank account records

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Egmont Group provides FIU-to-FIU intelligence sharing for financial intelligence analysis. MLATs provide formal, legally binding mechanisms for obtaining evidence (including bank records) admissible in criminal proceedings, particularly when banking secrecy laws prevent voluntary disclosure.

---

**Question B.3.2**
What is the primary operational objective of the Egmont Group, and how does it facilitate international anti-money laundering efforts?

A. It functions as an international court that prosecutes money laundering kingpins

B. It provides a secure platform for the exchange of financial intelligence and expertise among its member Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs) worldwide

C. It directly manages asset recovery operations for international corporate bankruptcies

D. It sets binding global tax rates to eliminate offshore tax avoidance schemes

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The Egmont Group provides a secure platform (Egmont Secure Web) for FIU-to-FIU information exchange and promotes cooperation and training among member FIUs worldwide.

---

**Question B.3.3**
Which two principles govern information exchange between Financial Intelligence Units under the Egmont Group framework?

A. Exchanged information must be used only for AML/CFT purposes

B. Information may be freely shared with the subject of the intelligence

C. The receiving FIU must maintain confidentiality of the shared intelligence

D. No limitations exist on how the receiving FIU may use the information

**Correct Answers: A and C (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Under Egmont Group principles, exchanged information must be used only for AML/CFT purposes and the receiving FIU must maintain confidentiality of the shared intelligence.

### B.4 Objectives of Public Sector Groups, Private Sector Groups, and Non-Governmental Organizations

---

**Question B.4.1**
The Wolfsberg Group's guidance on Correspondent Banking emphasizes that a correspondent bank should evaluate the respondent bank's AML control environment. According to these principles, which factor is considered most critical when evaluating a respondent's customer base?

A. The total volume of retail mortgage loans issued to local citizens

B. The presence of high-risk customer segments, such as downstream NBFIs, money services businesses (MSBs), and foreign PEPs

C. The type of accounting software utilized by the respondent bank's internal finance department

D. The physical architectural security of the respondent bank's regional headquarters

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The Wolfsberg Correspondent Banking Due Diligence Questionnaire focuses on identifying high-risk customer segments (MSBs, NBFIs, PEPs) that the respondent bank services, as these create downstream risk for the correspondent bank.

---

**Question B.4.2**
Public-private partnerships (PPPs) in AML primarily seek to achieve which objective?

A. Replace SAR filing obligations

B. Improve intelligence sharing and detection of complex financial crime networks

C. Eliminate KYC requirements

D. Centralize all investigations within law enforcement

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Public-private partnerships aim to improve intelligence sharing between government agencies (FIU, law enforcement) and private financial institutions to better detect and disrupt complex financial crime networks.

### B.5 How Regulatory Authorities Cooperate During Exams, and How Law Enforcement/FIUs Cooperate During Cross-Border Investigations

---

**Question B.5.1**
A financial investigator in Country A identifies that $10 million in stolen sovereign funds was wired into a private bank account in Country B. Country A and Country B have an active Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) in place. What is the function of the MLAT in this context?

A. It allows Country A to send its own police forces to physically arrest bankers in Country B without local authorization

B. It provides a formal, legally binding mechanism for the governments to cooperate in the exchange of evidence, judicial documents, and asset tracking/freezing across borders

C. It acts as an informal social network for regional intelligence sharing

D. It automatically synchronizes the tax codes of both nations

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** MLATs provide formal, legally binding mechanisms for cross-border exchange of evidence, judicial documents, and asset freezing—essential for international money laundering investigations.

---

**Question B.5.2**
An investigator at FIU Alpha identifies a suspicious cross-border wire sequence that terminates in the jurisdiction of FIU Beta. Can FIU Alpha share financial intelligence directly with FIU Beta, and under what standard principles?

A. No; FIU intelligence is completely proprietary and cannot cross border lines

B. Yes; they can exchange intelligence directly, typically adhering to the Egmont Group Principles for Information Exchange Between Financial Intelligence Units

C. Yes, but only if they obtain an explicit decree from the International Court of Justice

D. No, unless the transaction volume exceeds $1 billion USD equivalent

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** FIU-to-FIU intelligence exchange is permitted and encouraged under the Egmont Group Principles for Information Exchange, using secure channels such as the Egmont Secure Web.

### B.7 Guidance and Special Reports from Non-Government Bodies

---

**Question B.7.1**
A global correspondent bank is onboarding a new respondent bank and receives the respondent's Wolfsberg Correspondent Banking Due Diligence Questionnaire (CBDDQ). The CBDDQ reveals the respondent services 14 MSB customers; three MSBs offer unlicensed international remittance services; the respondent provides payable-through account access to 22 sub-agents; and the AML program was last audited four years ago. According to Wolfsberg principles, which factor represents the highest residual AML risk?

A. The absence of recent enforcement actions from the local regulator

B. Servicing of unlicensed MSBs and provision of payable-through access to undisclosed sub-agents, combined with a four-year audit gap

C. The moderate FATF rating of the respondent's home jurisdiction

D. Servicing of 14 MSBs, which is within acceptable industry range

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Under Wolfsberg principles, the highest risk factor is the combination of unlicensed MSBs, payable-through access to undisclosed sub-agents (which creates visibility gaps), and the lack of recent independent audit verification of controls.

### B.8 National and Sectoral Risk Assessments and Impact on Risk-Based Approach

---

**Question B.8.1**
A mid-sized regional bank is preparing its annual Enterprise-Wide Risk Assessment (EWRA). The assessment team calculates inherent risk scores: retail banking 6.2/10, commercial lending 4.1/10, private banking 8.7/10. After applying mitigating controls, residual risk scores are: retail 4.4/10, commercial 2.8/10, private banking 6.9/10. The methodology defines residual risk above 6.0 as "high residual risk" requiring board escalation. Which statement correctly describes the next required steps?

A. The EWRA is complete once residual scores are documented; no further action required

B. Both retail and private banking require board-level escalation

C. Only private banking requires board-level escalation because it is the only line with residual risk exceeding the 6.0 high-risk threshold; the board must be informed, independent audit must prioritize this line, and compliance must develop a risk mitigation plan

D. All three business lines require immediate account closure of all high-risk relationships

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Only business lines with residual risk above the defined high-risk threshold (6.0) require board-level escalation, independent audit prioritization, and a formal risk mitigation plan with defined timeframe.

---

**Question B.8.2**
A bank's Enterprise-Wide Risk Assessment (EWRA) must document which of the following?

A. Only the sanctions screening procedures for correspondent accounts

B. An assessment of the institution's inherent risk exposure across all products, services, customer types, geographies, and delivery channels, as well as the mitigating controls in place, resulting in a net residual risk determination

C. A list of all suspicious activity reports filed in the previous calendar year sorted by dollar amount

D. The board of directors' personal tax filings to assess insider risk

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** An EWRA must document inherent risk across all risk dimensions and the mitigating controls applied, resulting in a residual risk determination. This holistic assessment drives risk-based resource allocation.

### B.9 AFC Regulations and Main Regulatory Bodies in the US and EU

---

**Question B.9.1**
Under the U.S. Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) requirements (31 CFR §1020.210) as amended by FinCEN's 2016 CDD Final Rule, which correctly identifies the five minimum required pillars of a financial institution's AML compliance program?

A. Internal controls; a designated compliance officer; ongoing employee training; independent testing; customer due diligence (including beneficial ownership identification)

B. Annual board resolutions; a quarterly sanctions report; a law enforcement liaison desk; a digital asset custodian; automated wire screening

C. Simplified due diligence for all customers; waiver of PEP screening; outsourced monitoring; customer ombudsman; annual profit-sharing

D. Internal controls; designated compliance officer; outsourced customer onboarding; independent testing; monthly CTRs

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The BSA requires five pillars: (1) internal controls, (2) designated compliance officer, (3) ongoing employee training, (4) independent testing, and (5) customer due diligence (including beneficial ownership identification per the 2016 CDD Final Rule).

**Question B.9.2**
A European bank's legal team is reviewing whether a recent enforcement action against a corporate subsidiary triggers liability under the EU Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive (6AMLD). The subsidiary was found to have knowingly processed proceeds derived from tax fraud. Which statement most accurately describes the subsidiary's exposure under 6AMLD?

A. The subsidiary faces no criminal exposure because 6AMLD only harmonizes predicate offenses for natural persons

B. The subsidiary faces criminal liability for money laundering as a legal person under 6AMLD. The Directive harmonizes 22 predicate offenses including tax crimes, and explicitly extends criminal liability to legal persons, meaning the corporation itself can be prosecuted and subject to criminal sanctions

C. The subsidiary is only liable if individual directors are also convicted

D. Tax fraud was not included in 6AMLD's harmonized list of predicate offenses

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** 6AMLD explicitly extends criminal liability to legal persons (corporations). The Directive harmonizes 22 predicate offenses including tax crimes across all EU member states. Corporations face criminal sanctions including fines, debarment from public contracts, and dissolution.

---

**Question B.9.3**
Which two features distinguish the EU Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive (6AMLD) from 5AMLD?

A. 6AMLD harmonizes predicate offenses across all EU member states

B. 6AMLD lowers the threshold for cash payments to €1,000

C. 6AMLD eliminates all customer due diligence requirements

D. 6AMLD introduces criminal liability for legal persons (corporations)

**Correct Answers: A and D (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** 6AMLD introduces two major advancements: (1) harmonization of 22 predicate offenses across all EU member states, and (2) explicit criminal liability for legal persons (corporations).

**Question B.9.4**
Which two statements correctly describe U.S. requirements for Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) and structuring?

A. A customer depositing $9,000 on Monday, $9,000 on Wednesday, and $9,000 on Friday requires a CTR for the aggregate $27,000

B. Structuring itself is illegal regardless of whether the underlying funds are legitimate

C. Banks must obtain customer consent before filing a CTR

D. CTRs must be filed within 60 days of the transaction

**Correct Answers: A and B (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Banks must file CTRs for aggregate cash transactions exceeding $10,000 in a single business day. Structuring (deliberately breaking transactions to evade CTR requirements) is a federal crime independent of whether the underlying funds are legitimate.

### B.10 Importance of Awareness of AFC and Sanctions Regulatory Regimes in Differing Jurisdictions

---

**Question B.10.1**
A German bank processes a EUR-denominated loan of €45 million to a Turkish conglomerate for construction of a natural gas extraction facility in Iran. The construction contract is held by an Iranian state-owned energy company not on the OFAC SDN list. No U.S. dollars, U.S. persons, or U.S. financial system touch the transaction. The bank's legal team argues U.S. sanctions cannot apply because there is no U.S. nexus. Which statements correctly assess the bank's risk?

A. The legal team is correct; OFAC primary sanctions require U.S. nexus

B. The bank faces U.S. secondary sanctions risk under ITRSHRA; secondary sanctions can target non-U.S. entities conducting "significant" transactions with designated Iranian energy sector entities, restricting access to U.S. markets and correspondent banking

C. The bank is protected from secondary sanctions by the EU Blocking Statute

D. Practical risk exists because even with EU Blocking Statute, U.S. regulators can impose secondary sanctions restricting USD correspondent banking access

**Correct Answers: B and D (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Secondary sanctions can apply to non-U.S. entities without U.S. nexus, restricting access to U.S. markets and correspondent banking. The EU Blocking Statute provides a defense in EU courts but does not prevent U.S. regulators from imposing secondary sanctions that affect USD clearing access.

### B.11 Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs): Importance, Achievements, Strengths, and Limitations

---

**Question B.11.1**
Two banks each have accounts held by different individuals who appear to be part of the same money laundering network. Neither bank's internal investigation alone establishes the complete picture. Under FinCEN's Section 314(b) program, which statement accurately describes permissible information sharing?

A. Banks cannot share SAR-related information under any circumstances

B. Under Section 314(b), financial institutions that have filed the required opt-in notification may voluntarily share information with other opted-in institutions about persons suspected of ML/TF activity, receiving safe harbor from liability

C. Section 314(b) requires both institutions to first obtain a court order

D. Section 314(b) sharing is only permissible if suspected money laundering exceeds $1 million

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Section 314(b) allows opted-in financial institutions to voluntarily share information about suspected ML/TF activity with other opted-in institutions. The sharing institutions receive safe harbor from liability, and the purpose is to identify and report ML/TF activity.

**Question B.11.2**
What is the primary purpose of FinCEN Section 314(b)?

A. Mandatory SAR filing

B. Voluntary information sharing among financial institutions regarding suspected money laundering or terrorist financing

C. International sanctions enforcement

D. Beneficial ownership verification

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Section 314(b) facilitates voluntary information sharing among financial institutions to better identify and report potential money laundering and terrorist financing activities.

### B.12 How the Private Sector Can Foster Collaboration in Fighting Financial Crime

---

**Question B.12.1**
A financial institution receives a FinCEN 314(a) request identifying several individuals suspected of laundering proceeds from transnational organized crime. Which statement is most accurate?

A. The institution may disclose the request to the customer if no match is found

B. The institution must search only current customers

C. The institution must search accounts and transactions according to FinCEN instructions and maintain confidentiality of the request

D. The institution must automatically freeze any matching account

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Under Section 314(a), institutions must search their records as instructed by FinCEN, maintain confidentiality of the request, and report any matches. Freezing accounts is not automatically required; matches are reported to law enforcement.

### B.13 Other Laws and Regulations That Impact AML/CTF Applications (Data Privacy, Consumer Protection, ESG, Financial Inclusion)

---

**Question B.13.1**
A French bank's Data Protection Officer and BSA/AML Compliance Officer disagree. A customer submits a GDPR "right to erasure" request demanding deletion of all personal data including KYC documents, transaction records, and a SAR. The DPO argues GDPR right to erasure is absolute. The compliance officer argues AML recordkeeping obligations override the request. Which statement most accurately resolves the conflict?

A. The DPO is correct; GDPR is EU primary law and takes absolute precedence

B. The compliance officer is correct; GDPR Article 17(3) carves out exceptions where retention is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation under EU or member state law. AML/CFT recordkeeping obligations constitute such a legal obligation. The bank may lawfully refuse to erase records required for AML/CFT purposes

C. The bank must delete all records except the SAR itself

D. Neither obligation takes precedence; the bank must escalate to the European Data Protection Board

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** GDPR Article 17(3) explicitly provides exceptions to the right to erasure where retention is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation (including AML/CFT recordkeeping requirements). Additionally, SAR confidentiality prohibits acknowledging the existence of a SAR to the customer.

---

**Question B.13.2**
A bank is considering terminating all correspondent banking relationships with banks headquartered in Sub-Saharan African countries due to "elevated AML risk" following a regulatory action at a competitor institution. The compliance department has not conducted individual risk assessments of the 22 African correspondent relationships. Several of these banks have passed recent FATF mutual evaluations with "Compliant" or "Largely Compliant" ratings. What is the most significant regulatory risk created by this blanket termination mandate?

A. The mandate is legally permissible; banks have absolute discretion to choose correspondent partners

B. The blanket termination violates the risk-based approach, which requires individual assessments rather than categorical exclusions. Terminating compliant banks may drive customers to unregulated informal channels, contradict RBA principles, and create legal liability

C. The mandate creates no AML risk; terminating high-risk relationships always reduces ML exposure

D. The RBA allows regional-level risk assessments without individual entity analysis

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The RBA requires individual, documented risk assessments rather than blanket categorical exclusions. Indiscriminate de-risking may drive activity to less transparent channels, contradict RBA principles, and may create legal liability under anti-discrimination frameworks in some jurisdictions.

---

**Question B.13.3**
Which statement best describes the FATF and Wolfsberg Group guidance on de-risking?

A. Broad, categorical termination of entire classes of customers or geographic relationships is the preferred risk management strategy under the RBA

B. Financial institutions should conduct individual, documented risk assessments of respondent institutions rather than applying blanket exclusions based solely on geography or customer type; the RBA does not require zero-risk tolerance

C. Regulators require a zero-risk tolerance policy for all high-risk jurisdictions, making de-risking mandatory

D. De-risking is only a concern for correspondent banks; respondent institutions bear no resulting compliance obligations

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Both FATF and Wolfsberg Group guidance emphasize that financial institutions should conduct individual, documented risk assessments rather than applying blanket exclusions. The RBA does not require zero-risk tolerance; proportionate measures are acceptable.
