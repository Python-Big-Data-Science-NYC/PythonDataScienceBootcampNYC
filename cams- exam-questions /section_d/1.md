### D.1 Tools Available to Implement Controls Across the Customer Lifecycle

---

**Question D.1.1**
A bank is evaluating technology solutions to enhance its AML program. Which of the following tools is most effective for detecting previously unknown connections between accounts that may indicate a money laundering network?

A. Basic spreadsheet analysis of transaction volumes

B. Network analysis/link analysis tools that map relationships between accounts, counterparties, and transaction patterns to identify hidden clusters

C. Manual review of account opening documents

D. Single-name sanctions screening only

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Network analysis/link analysis tools can identify hidden relationships and clusters between accounts that may indicate coordinated money laundering activity, such as mule networks or circular transactions, which would be difficult to detect through manual review or basic analysis.

### D.2 Data Quality, Integrity, Access, Definitions, and Taxonomy

---

**Question D.2.1**
A compliance auditor identifies that customer identification numbers in the transaction monitoring system do not match those in the core banking system for 15% of high-risk accounts. What is the most significant risk created by this data quality issue?

A. Increased false positives only

B. Inability to link transactions to the correct customer, potentially allowing suspicious activity to go undetected or causing investigators to miss the full scope of a customer's activity across products

C. Higher storage costs due to duplicate records

D. Minor inconvenience for compliance staff

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Data quality issues—particularly mismatched customer identification numbers—can prevent linking transactions to the correct customer, causing investigators to miss the full scope of a customer's activity across products and potentially allowing suspicious activity to go undetected.

---

**Question D.2.2**
Which data issue creates the greatest AML monitoring risk?

A. Duplicate branch codes

B. Missing customer identification numbers preventing entity linkage across systems

C. Delayed statement generation

D. Inconsistent check numbering

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Missing customer identification numbers prevent the bank from linking a customer's activity across different products, accounts, and systems, creating a fragmented view that can hide suspicious patterns and prevent accurate customer risk assessment.

A. Eliminate all identity verification to provide frictionless onboarding

### D.6 Onboarding Controls: Name Screening/Customer Screening and Use of Sanctions Lists

---

**Question D.6.1**
A global payment processing network runs a daily sweep of cross-border transactions against the OFAC SDN list. The system flags a transaction where the sender's name is spelled "M-U-A-M-A-R K-A-D-H-A-F-I" but the official list contains "M-O-A-M-M-A-R Q-A-D-D-A-F-I." What analytical capability must the screening software possess?

A. Deterministic matching; the analyst must instantly delete the record

B. Fuzzy logic/algorithmic matching; the analyst must manually review the hit to determine if it is a true match or false positive before releasing funds

C. Exact-string matching; the analyst should ignore it because letters do not match precisely

D. Cryptographic hashing; the analyst must automatically forward the payment

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Sanctions screening systems must employ fuzzy logic or algorithmic matching capable of identifying phonetically similar and transliterated name variants. Exact-string matching would miss names with common alternative spellings or transliterations. Each hit requires analyst review to determine if it is a true match.

---

**Question D.6.2**
A compliance analyst at a U.S. payment processor is reviewing a wire transfer where the sender's name is listed as "Muammar Gaddafi." The OFAC SDN list contains "Moammar Qaddafi" with 48 alternative name transliterations. The system uses exact-string matching and did not generate an alert. What conclusions are correct?

A. The use of exact-string matching for Arabic-origin names with many common transliterations is an inadequate screening methodology; systems must employ fuzzy logic

B. The payment processor bears no liability because exact-string matching is legally defensible

C. The missed match constitutes a potential OFAC sanctions violation; the processor must file a Voluntary Self-Disclosure with OFAC and implement corrective action

D. The analyst should simply file a SAR and close the matter without reporting to OFAC

**Correct Answers: A and C (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** Exact-string matching for names with many transliterations is inadequate. The missed match is a potential OFAC violation requiring Voluntary Self-Disclosure and corrective action.

**Question D.7.1**
A bank is implementing a Perpetual KYC (pKYC) program. Which statement best describes the advantage of pKYC over traditional periodic refresh?

A. pKYC eliminates the need for any customer due diligence

B. pKYC continuously monitors customer activity and triggers CDD review when material changes occur (e.g., sudden change in transaction pattern, new high-risk counterparties, adverse media alerts), rather than waiting for a scheduled refresh cycle

### D.8 Ongoing Controls: Adverse Media Screening Tools

---

**Question D.8.1**
A bank's enhanced due diligence team is conducting adverse media screening on a corporate customer. They find: the CEO was named in a foreign parliamentary inquiry into a government procurement scandal (not charged); a company with similar name and same address was dissolved following a trade fraud investigation; the primary business counterparty's director appears on Interpol Red Notice. What is the correct AML response?

A. Because no conviction occurred and the account has been clean for four years, the adverse media is inconclusive

B. The three findings collectively constitute credible adverse media requiring immediate escalation: re-assessment of risk rating, EDD review, enhanced monitoring, and potential SAR filing. OSINT findings do not require a conviction to be actionable—reasonable suspicion is the standard

C. The Interpol Red Notice alone requires immediate account closure

D. The dissolved predecessor company is irrelevant because it was a separate legal entity

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Credible adverse media from reliable sources (parliamentary inquiry, trade fraud investigation, Interpol Red Notice) collectively requires escalation, risk re-assessment, EDD, and potential SAR filing. Conviction is not required for reasonable suspicion.

### D.11 Ongoing Controls: Traditional Transaction Monitoring (Rules-Based Approach, Customer Segmentation, Scenario Coverage, Threshold Setting, Statistical Testing, Model Risk Management)

---

**Question D.11.1**
An AML operations director reviews a transaction monitoring system validation that found 77% of historical SAR cases would not have generated alerts under current thresholds. The director argues this is acceptable because alert volumes are already high (22,000 per month). Which assessments are correct?

A. A 77% false-negative rate demonstrates the current calibration is systematically missing genuine suspicious activity, constituting a material deficiency

B. Operational manageability (alert volume) is a valid justification for maintaining thresholds that produce high false-negative rates

C. The monitoring system must be recalibrated; the bank should document findings, deficiencies, a remediation plan with timelines, and present to senior management and the board

D. A system generating 22,000 alerts per month is inherently effective regardless of false-negative rates

**Correct Answers: A and C (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** A 77% false-negative rate (missing known suspicious activity) is a material deficiency. The bank must recalibrate, document findings and remediation plan, and present to senior management. Operational burden is not a valid justification for ineffective monitoring.

---

**Question D.11.2**
A bank's automated transaction monitoring system generates 15,000 alerts per month, but 99.8% are closed as false positives. The compliance director raises all behavioral thresholds by 300% to reduce operational burden. What systemic risk does this create?

A. Extreme over-filtering, creating an unacceptable risk of missing actual structured money laundering activities (false negatives)

B. Increased volume of SAR filings to the FIU

C. Violation of international accounting principles

D. Automatic classification of all lower-tier staff as PEPs

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Raising thresholds too high creates over-filtering, which increases false negatives—genuine suspicious activity will not generate alerts and will go undetected.

---

**Question D.11.3**
Which two statements correctly characterize AML transaction monitoring calibration risks?

A. False positives occur when legitimate activity triggers an alert that is not suspicious

B. False negatives occur when suspicious activity fails to generate any alert

C. False positives are always preferable to false negatives because they cause no harm

D. Over-filtering (raising thresholds too high) increases false negatives

**Correct Answers: A, B, and D (SELECT TWO as required by question format—typically A and B, or A and D depending on specific question)**

**Explanation:** False positives = legitimate activity flagged as suspicious. False negatives = suspicious activity missed (most dangerous). Over-filtering increases false negatives.

**Question D.12.1**
A bank has deployed a machine learning-based transaction monitoring system using a deep neural network model with 82% accuracy (vs. 34% for prior rules-based system). However, the model operates as a "black box"—analysts cannot understand why transactions are flagged. A regulator rates model governance as "unsatisfactory." Why is high accuracy insufficient?

A. The regulator is incorrect; accuracy is the only metric that matters

B. Model explainability is a core governance requirement; analysts must understand why a transaction was flagged to complete meaningful investigations. An opaque model creates risks: cannot identify/correct biases, cannot validate effectiveness, regulators cannot assess legal soundness, and SARs based on unexplained outputs may not withstand legal scrutiny

C. The bank should file a SAR for every transaction the model flags regardless of understanding

D. Explainability applies only to sanctions screening models

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Model explainability is a core governance requirement. Analysts must understand why transactions are flagged to conduct meaningful investigations. Unexplained "black box" models cannot be properly validated, and SARs based on unexplained outputs may not withstand legal scrutiny.

**Question D.14.1**
A blockchain analytics firm provides a report to a regulated NFT marketplace showing Wallet Group A (17 wallets) purchased and resold 12 NFTs among themselves 89 times over 60 days, with prices escalating from $2,000 to $340,000. All wallets are funded from the same source wallet at a centralized exchange, where the KYC-verified account holder is Individual P. Individual P then sells one NFT to an unconnected third-party buyer for $310,000. Which findings are accurate?

A. The activity constitutes NFT wash trading—self-controlled wallets artificially inflate prices through circular transactions to create a fraudulent market record, then sell to an unwitting third party at the inflated price

B. The centralized exchange should file a SAR based on the wash trading pattern

C. Wash trading through NFTs is not a recognized money laundering typology

D. Individual P's conversion to fiat and reporting as capital gain fully satisfies all AML obligations

**Correct Answers: A and B (SELECT TWO)**

**Explanation:** NFT wash trading (circular transactions between self-controlled wallets to artificially inflate prices) is a recognized typology. The exchange should file a SAR based on the pattern.
